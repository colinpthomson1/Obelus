## Voice principles

### Plainspoken intelligence

Use direct, familiar language. Sound informed without sounding professorial.

### Calibrated certainty

Say what is known, how well it is supported, and what remains uncertain.

### Constructive curiosity

Invite a closer look. Never imply that disagreement proves dishonesty.

### Conversation-speed clarity

UI copy must be understood in a glance. Lead with the finding, then reveal depth.

### Warm composure

A little wit is welcome in marketing. Snark, triumphalism, and alarmism are not.

## Tone by moment

| Moment | Tone | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listening | Quiet and reassuring | Listening for checkable claims… |
| Checking | Specific and active | Comparing this claim across 6 sources… |
| Early finding | Calibrated | Early finding: published estimates vary. |
| Supported | Clear, never celebratory | Supported by the latest agency release. |
| Disputed | Factual, not accusatory | This conflicts with the latest published data. |
| Unresolved | Honest and useful | No reliable source has been found yet. |
| Error | Direct and helpful | The source could not be reached. Try this check again. |
| Correction | Transparent | Updated after a newer source was found. |

## Claim-state vocabulary

Use these labels consistently:

- Supported
- Disputed
- Needs context
- Unverified
- Outdated
- Sources conflict
- Opinion / not independently checkable
- No reliable source found yet

Avoid by default:

- Lie or liar
- Obviously false
- Debunked
- Truth score
- Hallucination
- Fact-check complete
- No evidence

## Before and after

**Avoid:** False. The speaker is wrong.  
**Use:** This claim conflicts with the latest published Census data.

**Avoid:** No evidence.  
**Use:** No reliable source has been found yet.

**Avoid:** Fact-check complete.  
**Use:** Checked against 8 sources. Last updated 2 minutes ago.

**Avoid:** The guest lied about the figure.  
**Use:** The stated figure does not match the latest published result.

## Writing mechanics

- Lead with the result, then the qualification, then the evidence.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Use sentence case throughout product UI.
- Keep buttons to a specific verb and object: “Check this claim,” not “Submit.”
- Show source dates and freshness in plain language.
- Reserve exclamation points for rare marketing moments, never findings or errors.
- Never use color alone to communicate a claim state.
- Avoid false precision in confidence percentages unless the methodology is defensible and explained.
