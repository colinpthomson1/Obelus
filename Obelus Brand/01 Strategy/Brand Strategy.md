# Obelus Brand Strategy

## Strategic foundation

**Category**  
Live verification

**Brand essence**  
Constructive skepticism

**Brand role**  
The calm layer between a claim and a decision

**Core promise**  
Sourced context while the moment still matters

**Mission**  
Make scrutiny a natural, constructive part of conversation.

**Vision**  
A world where evidence can keep pace with assertion.

## Positioning

For people who conduct consequential conversations, Obelus is the live verification layer that turns spoken claims into sourced context while the conversation is still happening. Unlike transcripts, search tabs, or post-call fact-checking, Obelus keeps the evidence inside the conversation, fast enough to use now and transparent enough to trust later.

## Short product description

Real-time fact-checking and research for spoken conversations. Obelus follows the transcript, surfaces checkable claims, and brings reliable sources into view automatically or when asked.

## The problem

The strategic enemy is not “the liar.” It is unchecked momentum: a claim is spoken, repeated, and turned into a decision before anyone has time to examine it.

Obelus should never behave like a courtroom or a scoring system for people. It examines a statement, makes its evidence legible, and leaves room for uncertainty and revision.

## Brand tensions to protect

| Balance | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Precision + humanity | Exact, sourced findings expressed in language people can absorb while listening |
| Skepticism + curiosity | A closer look, not an accusation |
| Speed + restraint | Responsive early findings that never pretend to be final |
| Authority + humility | Confidence earned through visible process and calibrated language |
| Minimalism + personality | A disciplined core system with warmth in motion, editorial devices, and voice |

## Priority audiences

### 1. Journalists, interviewers, podcast hosts, and live producers

They need to check names, dates, statistics, and assertions before the moment for a follow-up passes.

**Audience message:** Ask the next question while it still matters.

### 2. Researchers, analysts, consultants, investors, and diligence teams

They need to validate market claims, precedents, and supporting data during calls.

**Audience message:** Turn every conversation into a sourced research session.

### 3. Decision-makers in consequential meetings

Executives, procurement teams, policy teams, communications leaders, and operators cannot afford to build decisions on false premises.

**Audience message:** Do not let an unchecked claim become a decision.

### 4. Accuracy-accountable institutions

Education, civic organizations, media organizations, and other teams need a credible, reviewable record.

**Audience message:** Make the record easier to trust.

### 5. Curious individual users

People want a better second screen for interviews, debates, meetings, and live media.

**Audience message:** Do not just hear it. Check it.

The recommended launch wedge is audiences 1 and 2. Their need is frequent, the cost of a missed check is clear, and the product can be demonstrated visibly.

## Brand pillars

### Evidence over verdicts

Obelus earns trust by showing sources, dates, corroboration, contradiction, and uncertainty. It should never ask users to accept a black-box true-or-false badge.

- Product implication: Every conclusion opens into an evidence trail.
- Brand implication: Sources are prominent, not buried in footnotes.

### Fast, never reckless

The product operates at conversational speed while making provisional status visible.

- Product implication: Distinguish checking, early result, and verified across sources.
- Brand implication: Motion feels responsive and composed, never frantic.

### Question claims, not people

Obelus examines the statement without assigning motive to the speaker.

- Product implication: Label claims, not speakers.
- Brand implication: Avoid police, courtroom, siren, and “gotcha” metaphors.

### Make complexity legible

The first view gives the user what matters in seconds. The full research stays available underneath.

- Product implication: Finding first, concise explanation second, evidence trail third.
- Brand implication: Strong hierarchy, generous space, and disciplined status color.

### Trust made visible

Privacy, consent, provenance, corrections, and uncertainty are part of the product experience.

- Product implication: Show recording state, source freshness, conflicts, and revisions.
- Brand implication: Trust comes from visible process, not grand claims.

## Historical story

### Recommended short version

Obelus takes its name from an ancient editorial mark: a short horizontal stroke placed beside text whose authenticity was in question. The familiar division symbol is a later form associated with the same name. For us, it means one simple thing: look closer.

### Recommended full version

Long before the obelus became a division sign, ancient editors used a small horizontal mark in the margin to flag a line as doubtful or possibly spurious. The mark made uncertainty visible without hiding the words. Obelus brings that idea into live conversation, marking the moment a claim deserves evidence.

**Closing line:** Not a verdict. A reason to look closer.

### Accuracy note

- Obelus is the English/Latin form; obelos transliterates Greek ὀβελός.
- The Greek word originally referred to a spit or skewer.
- The earliest critical mark was essentially a horizontal stroke, not necessarily the modern dotted division sign.
- The familiar dotted form became a mathematical division sign much later.
- Do not say, “The ancient Greeks used the modern division sign to mark false text.”
- Diabolos is a different Greek word and is unrelated to this symbol.

## What the Stripe reference means

Borrow the rigor, not the surface styling:

- Lead with a plain category and outcome.
- Let real product UI prove the proposition.
- Build a flexible system rather than one-off assets.
- Keep the core identity disciplined and place personality in color, motion, data, and editorial moments.
- Engineer accessibility into the color system.
- Use short, specific copy and visible proof to create trust.

Do not copy Stripe's blurple palette, signature gradients, diagonal geometry, or typographic mannerisms. Obelus should feel warmer, more editorial, and more inquisitive.
