02 / POSITIONING

Evidence stays inside the conversation.

Unlike transcripts, search tabs, or post-call fact-checking, Obelus turns spoken claims into sourced context while the conversation is still happening.

01

Evidence over verdicts

Show sources, dates, corroboration, contradiction, and uncertainty. Never ask users to accept a black-box score.

02

Fast, never reckless

Make early findings visibly provisional. Conversational speed must not become false certainty.

03

Question claims, not people

Examine the statement without assigning intent or turning the room adversarial.

04

Make complexity legible

Finding first, concise qualification second, full evidence trail third.

05

Trust made visible

Consent, provenance, source freshness, conflict, and corrections are part of the experience.

03 / THE NAME

A reason to look closer.

Long before the obelus became a division sign, ancient editors used a horizontal mark in the margin to flag a line as doubtful or possibly spurious.

The familiar dotted division symbol is a later form associated with the same name. The brand uses that history poetically but accurately: the mark makes uncertainty visible without hiding the words.

“Not a verdict.
A reason to look closer.”

Write the name as Obelus and pronounce it OB-uh-lus. Obelos transliterates Greek ὀβελός. “Diabolos” is unrelated and should never appear in brand copy.

05 / VOICE

Clear. Calibrated. Curious.

Obelus sounds informed without sounding professorial and composed without becoming cold.

01

Plainspoken intelligence

Use direct, familiar language. Make complex evidence legible in a glance.

02

Calibrated certainty

Say what is known, how well it is supported, and what remains uncertain.

03

Constructive curiosity

Invite a closer look. Never imply that disagreement proves dishonesty.

04

Conversation-speed clarity

Lead with the finding, then the qualification, then the evidence.

05

Warm composure

A little wit belongs in marketing. Snark, triumphalism, and alarm do not.

Use

“This claim conflicts with the latest published data.”

“No reliable source has been found yet.”

Supported · Disputed · Needs context · Unverified

Avoid

“False. The speaker is wrong.”

“No evidence.”

Lie · Liar · Truth score · Obviously false

06 / LOGO

The Dialogue Axis.

Two offset dots suggest participants or independent checks. The horizontal stroke is the claim under examination. A soft editorial taper adds direction without becoming an arrow.

CLEAR SPACE + MINIMUM SIZE

Keep 1.5 dot diameters clear on every side. Use the responsive micro symbol below 20 px. Never substitute a typed division glyph.

DO NOT

05 / DETERMINATE

Progress Divide

Only for real measurable progress.

Calm scrutiny

No shake, bounce, elastic easing, alarm flash, or status color while a finding is unresolved.

Recognition first

Keep a faint base mark or a complete-mark hold so the symbol remains recognizable.

Accessible by design

Use text status, polite announcements, reduced motion, and forced-color support.

11 / APPLICATIONS

Let the product prove the promise.

The identity is designed first for the software experience and landing page, then extended into documents, presentations, social assets, and developer tokens.

12 / PRODUCTION

Everything needed to build.

Masters are vector, fonts are open source, motion is implementation-ready, and the system includes CSS, JSON, Tailwind, React, contrast, and manifest files.
